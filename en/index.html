Why Sindal

A music theory engine, not a black box

Sindal has learned nothing from existing music. It applies the rules of written composition (scales, degrees, voice leading, cadences) to the settings you choose. What comes out resembles no one else's work, and raises no question about training data rights.

🎼

The music is yours

No subscription, no watermark, no clause about what you do with it. Files land on your own disk, in open formats.

🔒

Nothing leaves your machine

No account, no server, no telemetry. Sindal runs entirely offline, sound synthesis included.

✍️

You stay in control

A bar you dislike? Generate three variations, hear them in context, graft in the one you prefer. Or open the editor and move notes one by one.

🎹

Sound is included

A complete General MIDI bank ships with the application: Sindal plays and exports audio from the moment it is installed. More about sound.

📤

Open formats

MIDI for your sequencer, WAV for editing, MusicXML for MuseScore or Dorico, PNG for a printable score.

⚡

No runtime to install beforehand, no administrator prompt. The application lands in your user folder and updates itself.

How it works

Four moves, from settings to file

Set

Nine moods (happy, sad, epic, celtic, oriental, neoclassical and more), twelve keys, the tempo, the number of bars. Then, if you want to go further: chord density, harmonic colours, the rhythm profile, rests, the dynamic arc, the register, the refrain.

Generate

The engine writes a melody, a left hand and a bass line that agree with each other: chords are diatonic by construction, the left hand never holds more than three notes, and the piece ends on a real cadence with a ritardando.

Listen, then rework

Select the bars that miss. Sindal offers three variations, playable in context, and grafts in the one you pick. You can also insert or remove a bar, copy one bar onto another, or open the editor and move notes individually.

Save

MIDI, WAV, a PNG score, MusicXML, and a .sindal project that reopens the piece exactly as you left it.

Sound

A sound bank included, and yours on top

A MIDI file holds no sound at all: it describes notes, and something has to play them. Sindal brings its own instrument, so the application sounds from the moment it is installed. No configuration, no external synthesiser, no connection.

🎛️

GeneralUser GS included

S. Christian Collins' General MIDI SoundFont (32 MB, 128 instruments) is installed alongside the application, and is enough to play and export everything you compose. Its licence allows musical use, commercial included.

📚

Your own banks

Load any .sf2 SoundFont or .sfz bank. A full sampled piano works, thanks to lazy loading. The bank you choose is remembered and reloaded on the next launch.

💾

Real audio export

Exported WAV is rendered by the active bank, not by a system synthesiser: what you hear in the application is exactly what lands in the file.

An .sfz bank describes a single instrument, so the instrument selector greys out and the application explains why. That is deliberate: the bank is the instrument.

Help

Frequently asked questions

Sound problem, instrument that won't change? Answers to the most common cases.

The sound quality is poor: how can I improve it?

The timbre comes from the active sound bank. The bundled one (GeneralUser GS) is deliberately all-round; a better-sampled bank sounds noticeably more realistic.

  1. Download a .sf2 SoundFont or a .sfz bank suited to the instrument you want (a sampled piano, for example).
  2. In the Settings tab, choose Browse for a .sf2 or .sfz bank.
  3. Listen again: the bank is remembered and reloaded at next start, and the exported WAV uses the same one.

To go back to the original sound: Restore the default bank.

How do I change instrument?

In the Settings tab, open the Instrument list (piano, harpsichord, harp, strings, flute…). The change is immediate: the piece is not recomposed, only the timbre changes.

The list is greyed out with a .sfz bank: it holds a single instrument. Load a .sf2 SoundFont to get the choice back.

I hear nothing: "No audio output"

No sound bank is loaded. Load a .sf2 or .sfz file from the Settings tab, or use Restore the default bank. Also check your system's audio output and the application's volume.

"Instrument missing from this SoundFont — silent"

The loaded SoundFont doesn't contain the chosen instrument. Pick another one in the Instrument list, or go back to the default bank, which covers all 128 General MIDI instruments.

My .sfz bank "produces no sound"

The .sfz file is read, but the samples (.wav, .flac) it references can't be found. Keep them in the bank's folder, with the original folder layout: absolute paths, or paths leading out of the bank's folder, are refused for safety.

My export has no WAV file

The WAV is rendered by the sound bank: with no bank loaded, only the other files are written. Load a .sf2 or .sfz bank, then save again.

Can the bass use a different instrument?

Yes, with the Pro offer: the Bass bank checkbox in the Settings tab. See the offers.
